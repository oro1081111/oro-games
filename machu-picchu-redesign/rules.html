Objective and key concepts

Founders of Machu Picchu is a shared-city building game for 3–5 players, ages 10+, with a play time of about 10–20 minutes.

Expand and stack tiles, establish Statues and Shrines, and assign Workers. At the end, add your Statue points, Shrine points, remaining-Pillar points, and most-Pillars bonus. The highest total wins.

Each turn, take 1 tile → place it → place 1 meeple → refill the display.
  • Each ordinary or Temple tile is one complete 1×2 tile. Its two spaces share one outer outline. Different contents within that outline belong to the same tile.
  • The Central tile is one complete 2×2 tile.
  • Empty table spaces are level 0. Tiles directly on the table are level 1, followed by levels 2, 3, 4, and so on.
  • The four terrains are Terraces, Pasture, Rocky Ground, and Water. A Temple space has no terrain.

This page covers the current web rules, including Pillar supports from level 0 and a full 6-point bonus for each player tied for most Pillars.

Components and setup

Components

  • 1 Central tile, with one space of each terrain in a 2×2 square.
  • 30 two-space tiles, comprising 16 Temple tiles and 14 ordinary tiles.
  • 35 meeples, 7 in each of 5 colors. Use the number required for your player count.
  • 30 single Pillars, 15 five-Pillar tokens, and 1 start-player marker. Pillar supply is unlimited.

Prepare the game

  1. Place the Central tile in the middle of the table. The web layout has Terraces at top left, Pasture at top right, Water at bottom left, and Rocky Ground at bottom right.
  2. Shuffle the Temple tiles and ordinary tiles separately into two face-down stacks.
  3. From left to right, reveal 2 Temple tiles and then 2 ordinary tiles to create the four-tile display. Put 1 Pillar on each ordinary tile.
  4. Choose player colors and distribute meeples and starting Pillars using the table below.
  5. For a physical game, the player who most recently visited a historic site starts. On the website, Player 1 starts the first round, followed by the other seats in order.

Playing on the website

  1. Select “New game”, choose 3–5 players, and set each seat to Local player, Computer, or Expert. Select “Start game”. Local players share this device and take turns in seat order.
  2. On your turn, select a display tile. All Pillars on it immediately enter your supply.
  3. Select “Rotate” to change orientation. Choose a dashed empty space or a tile marked with a green dot to set the first half. The direction marker points to the second half.
  4. Check the two spaces, resulting level, and Pillar cost. Select “Confirm build”. You can change the position or direction before confirming.
  5. Choose Statue, Priest, or Worker, select its location, and check the reward or cost before confirming. The display refills and play passes to the next player.
  • Scroll within the board to explore the city. Use “＋” and “−” to resize the board, and “Center” to recenter it.
  • A space’s top-right number is its level. The colored top-left meeple label shows its player number and role, with S for Statue, P for Priest, and W for Worker.
  • The “Turn log” lists completed builds and meeple placements.
  • Computer players choose each phase in sequence. Experts compare complete turns and use limited next-turn estimates for some candidates.
  • Progress saves in this browser. The Traditional Chinese game and English preview have separate saves. The rulebook opens in a new tab, so you can switch back to your game tab.

Tile faces use “Terrace” for Terraces and “Rock” for Rocky Ground. Their full terrain names appear in the accessible labels and display-tile hover text.

Take a display tile

Choose 1 of the 4 face-up tiles and collect that tile and every Pillar on it. These Pillars are available immediately for this turn’s building and Priest costs.

Slide the remaining tiles left, keeping their relative order. Add Pillars and draw the replacement only after your build and meeple action, following the refill procedure below.

An ordinary tile receives 1 Pillar when revealed. Further Pillars can accumulate on any display tile, including Temple tiles. The player who takes the tile collects all of them.

Place a tile and build supports

You can rotate your selected tile to any of four orientations. Its two spaces must be level with one another. You may never cover a meeple.

Expand at ground level

Place the whole tile on two empty table spaces at level 1. At least one space must share an edge with existing land. Corner contact alone is insufficient. This placement has no support cost.

Stack over existing tiles

If both spaces rest above tiles, the new tile must span two different existing tiles. Equal supporting levels allow free stacking onto the next level. If the levels differ, fill the lower side with Pillars.

Support one half from empty ground

At least one half must rest directly on an existing tile. The other half can extend onto empty ground, supported from level 0. Pay 2 Pillars for each missing level on the lower side. These supports stay permanently under the tile.

Support cost = difference between the original levels × 2 Pillars. New tile level = the higher original level + 1.

  • The first player can stack on the first turn. Cover one space of the Central tile and support the other half from empty ground. Pay 2 Pillars to place the new tile at level 2.
  • With empty ground under both halves, expand at level 1. You cannot raise both halves with Pillars.
  • You must afford the whole support cost before confirming the build.
  • A stacked tile need not share an edge with another tile at its new level.
  • You can cover an unoccupied Temple. Covered terrain and Temples are removed from the visible board for subsequent actions and scoring.

Meeple actions and enclosure

After building, you must choose one action below and place 1 of your meeples. Meeples remain permanently where placed. A Temple occupied by any meeple cannot be used again.

Statue

Stand a meeple on empty ground sharing an edge with the land. A Statue occupies level 0, with no tile above its space. At game end it scores the levels of the topmost tiles in its four orthogonally adjacent spaces.

You can place it in an open or already enclosed area. Placing it in an already enclosed area immediately grants 1 Pillar.

What counts as enclosed?

Viewed from above, follow connected empty-ground spaces using orthogonal steps. The area is enclosed when it has no path to the outside of the land. It may contain several spaces and several Statues. Statues do not block these empty-ground paths. A diagonal opening does not provide an orthogonal escape.

If your new tile encloses an existing Statue’s area for the first time, its owner and you each gain 1 Pillar, for each Statue enclosed. If both are you, gain 2 Pillars for that Statue. Collect this reward before the meeple action, so it can help pay for a Priest. Each Statue awards an enclosure reward once.

Priest

Choose any uncovered, unoccupied Temple. Pay Pillars equal to its level and stand a meeple on it to establish a Shrine. A level-3 Temple costs 3 Pillars. The Shrine scores its Temple effect at game end.

Worker

Choose any uncovered, unoccupied Temple and lay a meeple on it. Immediately gain twice its level in Pillars. A level-3 Temple grants 6 Pillars. This reward is paid once when the Worker is placed. That Temple is occupied and provides no Shrine points.

Priests and Workers can use any available Temple from an earlier turn. If no Temple is available, place a Statue. If you cannot afford a Priest, choose a Worker or Statue.

Refill the display

After both the build and meeple action, follow these steps in order.

  1. Slide the remaining tiles left, keeping their relative order.
  2. Add 1 Pillar to each of the two leftmost tiles, regardless of type.
  3. Draw 1 tile from the same type of stack as the tile you took and add it to the right end. If that stack is empty, use the other stack.
  4. If the new tile is ordinary, add 1 starting Pillar to it. A newly revealed Temple tile has 0 starting Pillars.

For example, start with Temple A (0), Temple B (0), ordinary C (1), ordinary D (1). After taking B and completing your turn, the display is Temple A (1), ordinary C (2), ordinary D (1), new Temple (0). Numbers in parentheses are Pillars.

Next round and game end

After everyone has taken one turn, compare the Pillars in all players’ supplies. The player with the most takes the start-player marker and starts the next round. The others follow in seat order.

If players tie for most, check clockwise from the current round’s start player, including that player. The first tied player encountered becomes the next start player.

For example, in a three-player game starting with Player 2, Players 1 and 3 tie for most. Check Players 2, 3, then 1. Player 3 starts the next round.

The game ends immediately after 7 rounds with 3 players, 6 rounds with 4, or 5 rounds with 5. All players then calculate final scores.

Full final scoring

Total = Statue points + Shrine points + remaining-Pillar points + most-Pillars bonus.

Statues

For each Statue, add the levels of the topmost visible tiles in its four orthogonally adjacent spaces. A direction with no tile contributes 0. This scoring applies to every Statue, whether enclosed or open.

Adjacent levels of 2, 3, 1, and 0 score 6 points. Score each Statue separately. A tile space may contribute to several Statues.

Shrines

Each Shrine scores its Temple effect value multiplied by its level.
TempleEffect value
SummitHighest level of any matching terrain space.
DomainSize of the largest connected matching terrain area, plus 1.
RegionsNumber of distinct connected matching terrain areas.
MonumentTotal number of your Statues.
ArtisanTotal number of your Workers, plus 1.
  • Count only topmost visible terrain spaces, including the Central tile. Temple spaces have no terrain. Covered terrain does not count.
  • Matching terrain connects through shared edges, including across different levels. Corner contact does not connect areas.
  • All players use the shared terrain board. Monument and Artisan use only the Shrine owner’s meeples.
  • Score every Shrine independently, including multiple Shrines for the same terrain. Temples occupied by Workers provide no Shrine points.
  • If no matching terrain exists, Summit and Regions have effect value 0, while Domain has effect value 1.

A level-2 Domain Shrine with a largest matching area of 6 spaces scores (6 + 1) × 2 = 14 points. A level-3 Monument Shrine whose owner has 4 Statues scores 12 points.

Remaining Pillars

Score 1 point per complete pair of remaining Pillars, rounding down. For example, 9 Pillars score 4 points and 10 Pillars score 5.

Most-Pillars bonus

Each player with the most remaining Pillars gains 6 bonus points. Every tied player receives the full 6 points. Add this bonus to the ordinary remaining-Pillar points.

If three players finish with 10, 10, and 7 Pillars, their Pillar points are 5, 5, and 3, and their bonuses are 6, 6, and 0.

For a complete example, 12 Statue points + 14 Shrine points + 4 points from 9 remaining Pillars + a 6-point bonus for tying for most Pillars totals 36 points.

Winner

The highest total wins. If the highest totals tie, all tied players share victory.

Frequently asked questions

Can I cover both spaces of a tile because they show different terrains?

They still belong to one physical tile. When both supporting spaces are tiles, the new tile must span two different tiles.

Can the first turn use supports from empty ground?

Yes. Cover one space of the Central tile and extend the other half over empty ground. Pay 2 Pillars to build at level 2.

Can I raise both sides with Pillars to gain an extra level?

At least one half must rest directly on an existing tile. Pillars fill the difference on the lower side.

Can a Priest use a Temple already occupied by a Worker?

Meeples remain permanently, so that Temple cannot be used again or covered.

What if one build encloses several Statues?

Resolve each Statue’s first enclosure separately. The builder may gain several Pillars.

Can I skip my meeple action?

You must place one meeple each turn. When no Temple is available, place a Statue.

What if everyone finishes with 0 Pillars?

Everyone ties for most and each receives the full 6-point bonus.

Do I gain the most-Pillars bonus when becoming start player?

The round-end comparison determines the next start player. Award the 6-point bonus at game end.

Does opening “New game” erase my current game?

Select “Back” in the setup dialog to resume your game. Selecting “Start game” creates a new game and updates this version’s saved progress in your browser.
